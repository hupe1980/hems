Six statutes arrived in three years

A household with a roof, a battery, a car, a heat pump and a hot-water tank is a small power station with legal obligations. Most energy managers treat that as paperwork. hems treats it as computation.

Every regulatory number in the workspace carries the document and clause it came from — [BK6-22-300 A1 4.5.2], [LPC-031] — and cargo xtask check-citations resolves all {{ config.extra.stat_rules }} of them against an index of primary sources, failing the build if one names a document the index does not carry.

Three planes, three cadences, one order of authority

A house is decided at three speeds, and confusing them is how energy managers go wrong. Every layer may only narrow what the layer above it allowed, so “the grid limit was respected” is a property of the structure rather than a code path somebody remembered to write.

  • The grid limit is a proven property, not a code path

    [A1 4.6 S. 3] requires a network operator’s reduction to beat market control, so it lives in a plane the optimiser cannot reach around — checked by a 1000-round property test over random households, measurements, plans and user overrides.

  • It runs the house when the cloud is gone

    Guard, arbiter, planner and solar model take time as a parameter and open no socket. A winter day with a § 14a event is a unit test that runs in milliseconds — and a device the manager can only limit is handed back to its own thermostat rather than held at zero.

  • A kilowatt-hour has a price, and it differs per device

    The plan carries the dual of each store’s own state equation, so “a reduction takes power from where it is worth least” is a decision rather than a sentence. It prices the § 14a ceiling itself, too.

  • Measured where the rule measures it

    § 14a limits what the controllable devices draw from the grid, so a discharging battery lends them headroom. § 9 EEG limits what leaves the connection point, so the household’s own consumption is headroom too. Applying either per device is the obvious implementation, and it is wrong in euros.

  • Missing measurements are not zeroes

    A controllable device the drivers cannot hear from is assumed to be drawing its nameplate; a silent inverter is assumed to be producing nothing. That is the only pair of guesses that cannot overstate a budget, and the verdict names every asset it had to guess for.

  • Shared limits are shared, not repeated

    The main fuse in both directions, every sub-board and the 4,6 kVA Schieflast limit go through one weighted max-min allocator and intersect. Bounding each asset by a shared limit individually is the mistake that looks right.

  • without optimisation

    The same day delivering the same service — car charged, house warm, shower hot — with no battery, a wallbox that starts on plug-in and ordinary thermostats, against the same weather down to the cloud at 12:19, and under the same grid rules. A saving computed any other way flatters itself.

  • saved, against …of it on the bill

    €2,09 against €3,39. The saving counts the battery life, the comfort and the service the plan actually spent. The bill alone is the flattering number every other system quotes.

  • …covered by the store

    [A1 2.3] in one number: kilowatt-hours the battery lent the controllable devices during the reduction, which never crossed the connection point and which the Festlegung therefore does not count.

  • relief from § 14a was worth

    The shadow price of the network operator’s own ceiling. Nothing here, because the store lends the headroom; €3,93/kWh on the same evening in a household with no store whose car would otherwise leave short. Aggregators price both at “30 % of nominal”.

  • The three forecast lines are the evidence for the money lines

    The planner is given only what six weeks of the box’s own metering could have taught it. --perfect-foresight shows what a saving quoted without that measures: €5,25 against €2,09 on this day.

What is different about the numbers

Four rules the whole workspace is built to keep, because each of them is a way a saving figure is routinely inflated.

  • Every cost the optimiser may spend is a cost the report charges

    Battery wear, comfort, curtailed production, and the service the plan decided not to deliver. A plan that leaves the car two kilowatt-hours short buys two kilowatt-hours less electricity, and the bill alone would call that a saving.

  • The forecast is allowed to be wrong

    The simulated day runs a seeded realisation the planner never sees — a cloud at 12:19, a roof delivering 92 % of its datasheet — and every day scores its own forecasts, in CRPS, beside the money.

  • The baseline obeys the same law

    The household the saving is measured against is not an unregulated one. Its Steuerbox turns each device down on its own [A1 4.4.a] and its roof is capped by § 9 EEG, because a comparison against a household nobody is allowed to be is an advertisement rather than a measurement.

  • Compliance is counted, not averaged

    One household in ten thousand that failed to respect a reduction is an incident with a name and a date. “99,99 % compliance” reads as success, so the fleet view carries breaches as a list of findings and never as a rate — and a day only reaches it as a signed CloudEvent.

Or use one crate on its own

The domain crates are independent and none of them does any I/O. hems-grid is the German grid rules; hems-tariff is what a kilowatt-hour costs; hems-flex is the household’s flexibility in S2.

use hems_grid::para14a::{ControlMode, SteuVe, minimum_power};
use hems_core::prelude::{AssetId, Fallgruppe, Power};

let devices = [
    SteuVe {
        assets: vec![AssetId::new("wallbox")?],
        fallgruppe: Fallgruppe::Ladepunkt,
        power: Power::from_kw(11.0),
    },
    SteuVe {
        assets: vec![AssetId::new("battery")?],
        fallgruppe: Fallgruppe::Stromspeicher,
        power: Power::from_kw(5.0),
    },
];

// 4,2 kW + (2 − 1) × 0,8 × 4,2 kW = 7,56 kW — the floor the
// network operator may not go below, [BK6-22-300 A1 4.5.2].
let floor = minimum_power(&devices, ControlMode::Ems);
assert!((floor.kw() - 7.56).abs() < 1e-9);
